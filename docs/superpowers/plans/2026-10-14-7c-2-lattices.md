# 7c-2 Implicit Lattices Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Add three lattice nodes to the implicit world, TPMS, Strut Lattice and Voronoi Lattice, with the Straight mapping only: each fills a region (an implicit solid) with an optional skin, and its cell size, wall thickness and strut radius can be graded by a 7b field.

**Architecture:** Three new `ImplicitSolid` cases (`.tpms`, `.struts`, `.voronoi`), each a value type that holds its settings as `Grading`s and is cut to its region's bounding box, so `bounds` is finite. Each has a `lipschitz` constant, a CPU evaluation (`CPUEvaluation+Lattices`, `Double`, the complete engine on every platform) and a Metal twin (`ImplicitShaderLibrary+Lattices` plus a generator in `ImplicitCompiler`); the sources depend on the tree's shape only, so type, form, cell kind, numbers and seeds are buffers and editing one never recompiles. The nodes (`CreatorNodes/Implicit/Lattices/`) intersect the lattice with the region and add the skin (`Shell(region, inward, skin) ∪ (lattice ∩ Offset(region, −skin))`) out of the existing combinators. `ImplicitBench`'s sheet becomes a gyroid.

**Tech Stack:** Swift 6.4 (strict concurrency), Swift Testing, SwiftPM, runtime-compiled Metal Shading Language. Foundation only in `CreatorImplicit` (portable, Errata D11). No third-party libraries.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-implicit-lattices-design.md` §3.4 (lattices), §4.1 (compilation), §6 (rows i-1 to i-4), §7 (testing) and its Errata D11 to D13, under the parent spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-metalcreator-vertical-slice-design.md` with all its Errata. Mappings (§3.5) are 7c-4's and out of scope. Repo: worktree `MetalCreator-7c-2`, branch `7c-2`.

**Base:** this plan is built on **7c-core's planned code, which is not merged yet** (plan `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-13-7c-core.md`, 19 task commits, 4,163 tests with 12 "Test run with" lines). ## Decisions this plan makes where the spec is silent

The spec says what each lattice is, not how its distance is made. These are the plan's, each pinned by a test, and the ones a person might disagree with are under "User decisions" in the hand-off.

1. **A lattice case is cut to its region's box.** A TPMS fills space, so `bounds` (a box that contains the surface) needs a box; each case holds `region: BoundingBox` and its value is `max(lattice, distance to the box)`, exactly as `isosurface` does. The node intersects with the real region and adds the skin.
2. **TPMS scale.** g is not a distance. Each type has a `wallScale`, the mean of |∇g| over a thin shell about g = 0, so `|g| × a ÷ (2π × wallScale) < t ÷ 2` is a wall whose mean thickness is `t` to first order. The factors were measured on a 300³ grid; the gyroid's is pinned by a volume test against Schoen's area 3.0915 a² (within 5%), and the benchmark's 100 mm cube came out at 99% of the predicted wall volume. A network is `g × a ÷ (2π × wallScale) − t ÷ 2`: one volume, grown by half the thickness.
3. **Cell size is a field, the pattern follows it.** Lattice coordinates are `p ÷ (size(p) × stretch)`, anchored at the world origin so editing the region does not move the pattern. The lipschitz bound grows with the field's slope times the region's distance from the origin (`LatticeCells.warp`), as the spec says it must.
4. **A vector cell size is a stretch.** The `cell size` socket is a `field` (a number or a 7b field, like every graded setting); a second input `cell stretch` (a vector, default 1, 1, 1) gives the proportions, normalised to geometric mean 1 so that `cell size` stays the cell's volume-equivalent edge. See User decisions.
5. **Strut lattices** use the 27-cell window the spec names. Each of the six unit cells lists every strut once (a strut belongs to the cell its midpoint is in; kelvin and diamond are built from their vertices and de-duplicated), struts reach at most 0.125 cell outside their cell, so a point sees every strut within 0.875 of a cell, and the value is capped at 0.85 of the smallest cell edge. `node blend` is the polynomial smooth minimum over the struts, and may be at most a quarter of the smallest cell. A strut radius of 0.85 of that cell or more fills the region, and the node warns.
6. **Voronoi is made window-independent.** With R the search radius (twice the mean seed spacing, the grid cell size) and dₖ the k-th nearest seed distance capped at R: Struts is `(d₃ − d₁) ÷ 1.5 − t ÷ 2` (zero exactly where three cells meet; 1.5 per millimetre from an edge of three equal cells, pinned by a triangular-lattice test), Foam is the smallest distance to a plane half way to another seed, capped at `(R − d₁) ÷ 2`, minus `t ÷ 2`. The caps make the value the same whichever 27 cells are looked at, so it is continuous across grid cells and its lipschitz constant is a true bound (1 for foam, 2 ÷ 1.5 for struts). A point with no seed within R reads as on a wall (solid), and the node warns "The seeds leave gaps in the region; the lattice is solid there."
7. **Seeds.** `seeds` is a plane list socket (a vector wires in as a plane), so any 7a/7b point source works; each item's origin is a seed. Unconnected, `count` seeds are drawn by `SeededRandom(seed:, purpose: .voronoi)` (new purpose, salt "Voronoi_", pinned from an independent SplitMix64), each kept with the probability the `density` field gives (clamped to 0 ... 1), in a box the region's size plus half the mean spacing all round.
8. **No `mapping` input yet.** Mappings are 7c-4's; the lattice nodes have no `mapping` socket until then (the nodes' `evaluate` is Straight). 7c-4 adds the socket and a coordinate transform in front of the lattice functions.
9. **Resolution warnings.** The lattice node does not know the Mesh node's resolution. A preview run judges against the first preview pass (`PreviewSchedule(.automatic).firstResolution(for: region)`, 128 cells across), an export run against `MeshNode.defaultExportResolution` (0.1 mm), and the texts say "preview" or "export". Cells under two voxels and walls (or strut diameters) under one voxel warn; a graded thickness or radius below 0 warns through `ImplicitGrading` ("“thickness” is below 0 in places; it is held at 0 there.") and is clamped.
10. **Lipschitz bounds are conservative, from measured maxima.** `TPMSType.gradientBound` is the largest |∇g| found on a 160³ grid plus 5% (the P's is exact, √3); a test samples a grid and checks that every bound and `wallScale` holds.

## Review Focus

Input classes the spec implies and no obvious test covers; each has a test in Task 8 (`LatticeEdgeCaseTests`) unless noted.

1. **A skin thicker than half the region** (the offset interior is empty): the lattice vanishes and the result is the skin; no crash, no hole.
2. **A cell larger than the region, and a region far from the origin:** the box cuts a piece of one cell and the mesh is still watertight; a region 5,000 mm from the origin evaluates and, with a graded cell size, raises the lipschitz constant as the distance does.
3. **Walls and struts thinner than the mesh resolution:** the mesh stays watertight and manifold (marching cubes guarantees it), and the node warned before it was made.
4. **A lattice with nothing in the region** (struts of radius 0 far from every strut; a single Voronoi seed, which has no edges and no walls): the Mesh node says "There is no surface to mesh: the shape is empty, or smaller than a voxel." and nothing crashes.
5. **Seeds that are awkward:** all in one plane (meshes watertight), duplicated, a single one, far outside the region (the gap warning), a plane's origin as a seed. Task 6's node tests cover duplicates, the plane and the gap warning; Task 8 meshes a planar set and the far seed.
6. **A strut radius past the cells:** the radius range (0 to 5 mm) and the cell size range (1 mm and up) allow a radius of 2 mm in 2 mm cells, past `StrutLattice.reachInCells` (0.85) of a cell, where `min(nearest, reach) - radius` is negative everywhere. The node warns "Struts are thicker than the cells; the lattice is solid." (Task 5 tests the threshold and the stretch; Task 8 meshes the solid cube). The warning is a floor, not an exact test: a cubic lattice already reads solid from 0.71 of a cell, where the farthest point from a strut is.

import CreatorGeometry
import Foundation

/// How big a lattice's cells are (implicit spec §3.4): a cell size in millimetres that a field may grade, and a stretch that makes the
/// cells longer or shorter along an axis. The cell size is the geometric mean of the three cell edges and the stretch has geometric
/// mean 1, so a stretch changes a cell's shape and not its volume.
///
/// The lattice coordinates of a point are `p / (size(p) × stretch)`, so where the field changes fast the pattern stretches with it
/// (implicit spec §3.4). The pattern is anchored at the world origin, which keeps it still when the region is edited.
public struct LatticeCells: Hashable, Sendable {
    /// The cell size at each point, in millimetres.
    public var size: Grading
    /// The factor on each axis, with geometric mean 1.
    public var stretch: Vector3

    /// - Parameter stretch: the proportions of a cell; scaled to geometric mean 1. A component that is not a positive number counts
    ///   as 1.
    public init(size: Grading, stretch: Vector3 = Vector3(1, 1, 1)) {
        self.size = size
        func positive(_ value: Double) -> Double { value.isFinite && value > 0 ? value : 1 }
        let (x, y, z) = (positive(stretch.x), positive(stretch.y), positive(stretch.z))
        let mean = cbrt(x * y * z)
        self.stretch = Vector3(x / mean, y / mean, z / mean)
    }

    /// The smallest cell size the grading takes, never below a micrometre (the evaluator guards the division with it).
    public var smallest: Double { max(size.range.lowerBound, Self.minimumSize) }

    /// The smallest stretch factor.
    public var smallestStretch: Double { min(stretch.x, stretch.y, stretch.z) }

    /// The smallest a cell size is taken to be, in millimetres.
    public static let minimumSize = 1e-6

    /// The distance from the world origin to the farthest corner of `box`: the lattice coordinates `p / size(p)` move by up to this
    /// much times the size field's slope per millimetre, which is what a graded size adds to the lipschitz constant.
    public static func reach(of box: BoundingBox) -> Double {
        let corners = [box.min, box.max]
        return corners.flatMap { a in corners.flatMap { b in corners.map { c in Vector3(a.x, b.y, c.z).length } } }.max() ?? 0
    }

    /// 1 plus how much the size field's change moves a lattice coordinate, over a region at most `reach` from the origin: the factor
    /// the distance to the pattern's surface is multiplied by (implicit spec §3.4: "the lipschitz bound grows with the field's slope").
    public func warp(reach: Double) -> Double { 1 + reach * size.slope / smallest }
}

import CreatorGeometry

/// A triply periodic minimal surface lattice filling `region`'s box (implicit spec §3.4). Its value is
/// `max(wall, distance to the region box)`, where the wall is `|core| − thickness / 2` for a sheet and `core − thickness / 2` for a
/// network, and `core = g(2π p / (size × stretch)) × size / (2π × wallScale)` is g scaled to millimetres. The TPMS node intersects it
/// with the real region and adds the skin; this is only the lattice, cut off at the region's box so that its bounds are finite.
public struct TPMSLattice: Hashable, Sendable {
    public var type: TPMSType
    public var form: TPMSForm
    public var cells: LatticeCells
    /// The wall thickness (sheet) or the offset of the level (network), in millimetres; a negative value counts as 0.
    public var thickness: Grading
    /// The box the lattice is cut to.
    public var region: BoundingBox

    public init(type: TPMSType, form: TPMSForm, cells: LatticeCells, thickness: Grading, region: BoundingBox) {
        self.type = type
        self.form = form
        self.cells = cells
        self.thickness = thickness
        self.region = region
    }

    /// A constant L ≥ 1 with |f(a) − f(b)| ≤ L · |a − b|. Differentiating `core` gives (|∇g| ÷ wallScale) ÷ the smallest stretch
    /// times `cells.warp` for the moving lattice coordinates, plus |g| × the size field's slope ÷ (2π × wallScale) for the changing
    /// scale, plus half the thickness field's slope.
    public var lipschitz: Double {
        let warp = cells.warp(reach: LatticeCells.reach(of: region))
        let moving = type.gradientBound / (type.wallScale * cells.smallestStretch) * warp
        let scaling = type.valueBound * cells.size.slope / (2 * Double.pi * type.wallScale)
        return max(1, moving + scaling + thickness.slope / 2)
    }
}

import Foundation
import CreatorGeometry

/// The five triply periodic minimal surfaces of the TPMS lattice (implicit spec §3.4), each by its standard trigonometric
/// approximation g(q) = 0 with q in radians, period 2π on every axis. A cell of `a` millimetres has q = 2π p / a.
///
/// g is not a distance. Near its zero set a step of `h` in g is a step of about `h / |∇g|` radians, and |∇g| varies over the surface,
/// so each type has a `wallScale`: the mean of |∇g| over a thin shell about the surface. A wall `|g| < wallScale × t × π / a` then has
/// the mean thickness `t` to first order, which the tests pin (a gyroid's within 5%). `gradientBound` and `valueBound` are what the
/// lipschitz constant is made from; the tests check them against dense samples.
public enum TPMSType: Int, Hashable, Sendable, CaseIterable {
    case gyroid = 0
    case schwarzP = 1
    case diamond = 2
    case neovius = 3
    case lidinoid = 4

    /// The names the node's segmented control shows, in raw-value order.
    public static let options = ["Gyroid", "Schwarz P", "Diamond", "Neovius", "Lidinoid"]

    public var name: String { Self.options[rawValue] }

    /// g at `q` (radians).
    public func value(at q: Vector3) -> Double {
        let (sx, sy, sz) = (sin(q.x), sin(q.y), sin(q.z))
        let (cx, cy, cz) = (cos(q.x), cos(q.y), cos(q.z))
        switch self {
        case .gyroid:
            return sx * cy + sy * cz + sz * cx
        case .schwarzP:
            return cx + cy + cz
        case .diamond:
            return sx * sy * sz + sx * cy * cz + cx * sy * cz + cx * cy * sz
        case .neovius:
            return 3 * (cx + cy + cz) + 4 * cx * cy * cz
        case .lidinoid:
            let first = sin(2 * q.x) * cy * sz + sin(2 * q.y) * cz * sx + sin(2 * q.z) * cx * sy
            let second = cos(2 * q.x) * cos(2 * q.y) + cos(2 * q.y) * cos(2 * q.z) + cos(2 * q.z) * cos(2 * q.x)
            return 0.5 * first - 0.5 * second + 0.15

/// The Metal Shading Language of the lattices (implicit spec §3.4), after the shared library. Each function is a line-for-line port of
/// its twin in `CPUEvaluation+Lattices`; the agreement suite holds the two to 1e-4 mm. Trigonometry is `precise::` and its argument
/// is reduced to one period first, because the default fast sine is not accurate at the large arguments a small cell gives.
extension ImplicitShaderLibrary {
    static let lattices = """
    // MARK: TPMS lattices (TPMSLattice)

    inline float tpmsRaw(int type, float3 q) {
        float3 s = precise::sin(q), c = precise::cos(q);
        if (type == 0) { return s.x * c.y + s.y * c.z + s.z * c.x; }
        if (type == 1) { return c.x + c.y + c.z; }
        if (type == 2) { return s.x * s.y * s.z + s.x * c.y * c.z + c.x * s.y * c.z + c.x * c.y * s.z; }
        if (type == 3) { return 3.0 * (c.x + c.y + c.z) + 4.0 * c.x * c.y * c.z; }
        float first = precise::sin(2.0 * q.x) * c.y * s.z + precise::sin(2.0 * q.y) * c.z * s.x + precise::sin(2.0 * q.z) * c.x * s.y;
        float second = precise::cos(2.0 * q.x) * precise::cos(2.0 * q.y) + precise::cos(2.0 * q.y) * precise::cos(2.0 * q.z)
            + precise::cos(2.0 * q.z) * precise::cos(2.0 * q.x);
        return 0.5 * first - 0.5 * second + 0.15;
    }

    inline float tpmsLattice(float3 p, float cell, float3 stretch, float thickness, int type, int form, float wallScale,
                             float3 centre, float3 halfSize) {
        const float twoPi = 6.28318530717958647692;
        float size = max(cell, 0.000001);
        float3 u = p / (size * stretch);
        float3 q = twoPi * (u - floor(u));
        float core = tpmsRaw(type, q) * size / (twoPi * wallScale);
        float wall = max(thickness, 0.0);
        float body = form == 0 ? abs(core) - wall * 0.5 : core - wall * 0.5;
        return max(body, regionDistance(p, centre, halfSize));
    }
    """
}

/// The unit cells of the strut lattice (implicit spec §3.4): a set of straight struts in a cell of side 1, repeated in every direction.
/// Each cell lists every strut of the periodic lattice exactly once: a strut belongs to the cell its midpoint is in, so the lists have
/// no duplicates, and a strut may reach up to 0.125 of a cell beyond its own (the struts that cross cell faces).
public enum StrutCell: Int, Hashable, Sendable, CaseIterable {
    /// The cube's edges: 3 struts of length 1.
    case cubic = 0
    /// The centre to the 8 corners: 8 struts of length √3/2.
    case bcc = 1
    /// The face diagonals: each corner to the centres of the faces next to it, 12 struts of length √2/2.
    case fcc = 2
    /// The face diagonals and the edges of the octahedron between the face centres: 24 struts of length √2/2, the octet truss.
    case octet = 3
    /// The edges of truncated octahedra packed in a body-centred arrangement: 24 struts of length √2/4.
    case kelvin = 4
    /// The bonds of the diamond cubic structure: 16 struts of length √3/4.
    case diamond = 5

    /// The names the node's segmented control shows, in raw-value order.

    public var name: String { Self.options[rawValue] }

    /// One strut, from `start` to `end`, in cell coordinates (a cell is 0 ... 1 on each axis).
    public struct Strut: Hashable, Sendable {
        public var start: Vector3
        public var end: Vector3
    }

    /// The struts of this cell.
    public var struts: [Strut] { Self.table[rawValue] }

    /// How far, in cells, a strut of any cell reaches beyond the cell its midpoint is in.
    public static let spill = 0.125

    private static let table: [[Strut]] = allCases.map { Self.canonical(Self.raw($0)) }

    // MARK: Building the lists

    private static func raw(_ cell: StrutCell) -> [Strut] {
        switch cell {
        case .cubic:
            return [Vector3.unitX, .unitY, .unitZ].map { Strut(start: .zero, end: $0) }
        case .bcc:
            let centre = Vector3(0.5, 0.5, 0.5)
            return corners.map { Strut(start: centre, end: $0) }
        case .fcc:
            return faceDiagonals
        case .octet:

        case .kelvin:

            return truncatedOctahedron(about: origin) + truncatedOctahedron(about: centre)
        case .diamond:
            let atoms = [Vector3.zero, Vector3(0.5, 0.5, 0), Vector3(0.5, 0, 0.5), Vector3(0, 0.5, 0.5)]
            let bonds = [Vector3(1, 1, 1), Vector3(1, -1, -1), Vector3(-1, 1, -1), Vector3(-1, -1, 1)].map { $0 * 0.25 }
            return atoms.flatMap { atom in bonds.map { Strut(start: atom, end: atom + $0) } }
        }
    }

    private static let corners: [Vector3] = (0..<8).map { Vector3(Double($0 & 1), Double(($0 >> 1) & 1), Double($0 >> 2)) }

    private static let faceCentres: [Vector3] = [
        Vector3(0, 0.5, 0.5), Vector3(1, 0.5, 0.5), Vector3(0.5, 0, 0.5), Vector3(0.5, 1, 0.5), Vector3(0.5, 0.5, 0),
        Vector3(0.5, 0.5, 1),
    ]

    /// Each corner to the centres of the three faces it lies on, over the faces at x, y or z = 0 (the others belong to the next cell).
    private static var faceDiagonals: [Strut] {
        let near = [(faceCentres[0], 0), (faceCentres[2], 1), (faceCentres[4], 2)]
        return near.flatMap { centre, axis in
            corners.filter { [$0.x, $0.y, $0.z][axis] == 0 }.map { Strut(start: $0, end: centre) }
        }
    }

    /// Between the centres of faces that are not opposite: the twelve edges of the octahedron.
    private static var octahedronEdges: [Strut] {
        var edges: [Strut] = []
        for (index, first) in faceCentres.enumerated() {
            for second in faceCentres[(index + 1)...] where (first - second).length < 0.71 {
                edges.append(Strut(start: first, end: second))
            }
        }
        return edges
    }

    /// The 36 edges of a truncated octahedron of vertices (0, ±1, ±2) and permutations, scaled by ¼, about `centre`.
    private static func truncatedOctahedron(about centre: Vector3) -> [Strut] {
        let permutations = [[0, 1, 2], [0, 2, 1], [1, 0, 2], [1, 2, 0], [2, 0, 1], [2, 1, 0]]
        var vertices = Set<Vector3>()
        for order in permutations {
            for signs in 0..<4 {
                let magnitudes = [0.0, 1.0, 2.0]
                var v = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
                for (slot, axis) in order.enumerated() {
                    let sign = slot == 0 ? 1.0 : (slot == 1 ? (signs & 1 == 0 ? 1.0 : -1.0) : (signs & 2 == 0 ? 1.0 : -1.0))
                    v[axis] = sign * magnitudes[slot]
                }
                vertices.insert(Vector3(v[0], v[1], v[2]))
            }
        }
        let ordered = vertices.sorted { ($0.x, $0.y, $0.z) < ($1.x, $1.y, $1.z) }
        var edges: [Strut] = []
        for (index, first) in ordered.enumerated() {
            for second in ordered[(index + 1)...] where abs((first - second).length - 2.0.squareRoot()) < 1e-9 {
                edges.append(Strut(start: centre + first * 0.25, end: centre + second * 0.25))
            }
        }
        return edges
    }

    /// Moves every strut by whole cells so its midpoint is in [0, 1)³, and keeps one of each.
    private static func canonical(_ struts: [Strut]) -> [Strut] {
        var seen = Set<[Int]>()
        var result: [Strut] = []
        for strut in struts {
            let middle = (strut.start + strut.end) * 0.5
            let shift = Vector3(floor(middle.x + 1e-9), floor(middle.y + 1e-9), floor(middle.z + 1e-9))

            let key = ends.flatMap { [$0.x, $0.y, $0.z] }.map { Int(($0 * 1_000_000).rounded()) }
            if seen.insert(key).inserted { result.append(Strut(start: ends[0], end: ends[1])) }
        }
        return result
    }

import CreatorGeometry

/// A lattice of straight struts of radius `radius` filling `region`'s box (implicit spec §3.4). Its value is the distance to the
/// nearest strut less the radius, where the struts are those of the current cell and its 26 neighbours (a strut crosses cell faces),
/// capped at 0.85 of the smallest cell edge, which is as far as that window sees. `blend` rounds the joints with the polynomial smooth
/// minimum. Cut to the region's box.
public struct StrutLattice: Hashable, Sendable {
    public var cell: StrutCell
    public var cells: LatticeCells
    /// The strut radius in millimetres; a negative value counts as 0.
    public var radius: Grading
    /// The width of the smooth minimum over the struts, 0 for sharp joints.
    public var blend: Double
    public var region: BoundingBox

    /// How far, in cells, the window of 27 cells sees from any point: every strut outside it is at least `1 - StrutCell.spill`
    /// cells away, and the value is capped a little short of that.
    public static let reachInCells = 0.85

    public init(cell: StrutCell, cells: LatticeCells, radius: Grading, blend: Double, region: BoundingBox) {
        self.cell = cell
        self.cells = cells
        self.radius = radius
        self.blend = blend
        self.region = region
    }

    /// The distance to the lattice is 1-Lipschitz for a fixed cell size; a graded size moves the struts by their coordinates times the
    /// size field's slope over the size (`LatticeCells.warp`, with the window's reach added to the region's), and the radius field's
    /// slope is added.
    public var lipschitz: Double {
        let largest = max(cells.size.range.upperBound, cells.smallest) * max(cells.stretch.x, cells.stretch.y, cells.stretch.z)
        let warp = cells.warp(reach: LatticeCells.reach(of: region) + 2 * largest)
        return max(1, warp + radius.slope)
    }
}

import CreatorGeometry

/// A Voronoi lattice of `seeds` filling `region`'s box (implicit spec §3.4). With R the search radius and d₁ ≤ d₂ ≤ d₃ the distances
/// to the three nearest seeds, all of them capped at R:
/// - **Struts**: (d₃ − d₁) ÷ `strutScale` − thickness ÷ 2. d₃ − d₁ is 0 exactly where three cells meet (the cell edges), and grows
///   1.5 per millimetre away from an edge where three equal cells meet at 120°, which is `strutScale`.
/// - **Foam**: the distance to the nearest cell wall, the smallest over the other seeds of the distance to the plane half way to
///   them, capped at (R − d₁) ÷ 2 (a wall farther away than that may belong to a seed beyond the search), minus thickness ÷ 2.
/// Both are window-independent: a point with no seed within R reads as on a wall (solid), and the node warns about such gaps.
public struct VoronoiLattice: Hashable, Sendable {
    /// d₃ − d₁ grows by 1.5 per millimetre from an edge of three equal cells: the divisor that turns it into millimetres.
    public static let strutScale = 1.5

    public var seeds: VoronoiSeeds
    public var form: VoronoiForm
    /// The strut diameter or the wall thickness, in millimetres; a negative value counts as 0.
    public var thickness: Grading
    public var region: BoundingBox

    public init(seeds: VoronoiSeeds, form: VoronoiForm, thickness: Grading, region: BoundingBox) {
        self.seeds = seeds
        self.form = form
        self.thickness = thickness
        self.region = region
    }

    /// The distance to a wall is 1-Lipschitz (a minimum of distances to planes, capped by a half-slope term); d₃ − d₁ is 2-Lipschitz;
    /// the thickness field adds half its slope.
    public var lipschitz: Double {
        let base = switch form {
        case .struts: 2 / Self.strutScale
        case .foam: 1.0
        }
        return max(1, base + thickness.slope / 2)
    }
}

import CreatorGeometry
import Foundation

/// The seeds of a Voronoi lattice on a uniform grid (implicit spec §3.4): cells of `radius` millimetres, so the 27 cells around a point
/// hold every seed within `radius` of it. Immutable; equal by identity, like `DistanceBlocks`, so a tree with thousands of seeds is
/// cheap to compare.
public final class VoronoiSeeds: Hashable, Sendable {
    /// The most grid cells there are; a larger grid is made with larger cells.
    public static let maximumCells = 1 << 21

    public let points: [Vector3]
    /// The grid's cell size, which is also how far from a point the evaluation looks for seeds.
    public let radius: Double
    /// The corner of cell (0, 0, 0).
    public let origin: Vector3
    /// Cells along x, y and z.
    public let counts: [Int]
    /// Where each cell's seeds start in `entries`: cell (x fastest, then y, then z) `c` owns `entries[starts[c] ..< starts[c + 1]]`.
    public let starts: [Int32]
    /// Seed numbers, grouped by cell.
    public let entries: [Int32]

    /// - Parameters:
    ///   - points: the seeds, at least one, all finite.
    ///   - radius: how far to look for seeds from a point, at least this; made larger if the grid would pass `maximumCells`.
    public init(points: [Vector3], radius requested: Double) {
        precondition(!points.isEmpty && requested > 0, "VoronoiSeeds needs a seed and a radius")
        let box = BoundingBox(points: points) ?? BoundingBox(min: .zero, max: .zero)
        var size = requested
        func counts(_ size: Double) -> [Int] {
            [box.size.x, box.size.y, box.size.z].map { Int(($0 / size).rounded(.down)) + 1 }
        }
        while counts(size).reduce(1, *) > Self.maximumCells { size *= 1.5 }
        self.points = points
        self.radius = size
        self.origin = box.min
        let found = counts(size)
        self.counts = found
        var cellOf: [Int] = []
        cellOf.reserveCapacity(points.count)
        var tally = [Int32](repeating: 0, count: found[0] * found[1] * found[2] + 1)
        for point in points {
            let (x, y, z) = (min(Int((point.x - box.min.x) / size), found[0] - 1), min(Int((point.y - box.min.y) / size), found[1] - 1),
                             min(Int((point.z - box.min.z) / size), found[2] - 1))
            let cell = (z * found[1] + y) * found[0] + x
            cellOf.append(cell)
            tally[cell + 1] += 1
        }
        for index in 1..<tally.count { tally[index] += tally[index - 1] }
        var fill = tally
        var sorted = [Int32](repeating: 0, count: points.count)
        for (seed, cell) in cellOf.enumerated() {
            sorted[Int(fill[cell])] = Int32(seed)
            fill[cell] += 1
        }
        self.starts = tally
        self.entries = sorted
    }

    /// The search radius for `count` seeds spread through `region`: twice the mean spacing, so a point with no seed within the
    /// radius is a hole that Poisson-like seeds essentially never leave.
    public static func searchRadius(count: Int, in region: BoundingBox) -> Double {
        let volume = max(region.size.x, 1e-3) * max(region.size.y, 1e-3) * max(region.size.z, 1e-3)
        return 2 * cbrt(volume / Double(max(count, 1)))
    }

    /// Calls `visit` with the number and position of every seed in the 27 grid cells around `p`: all seeds within `radius` of `p`, and
    /// some farther. A point outside the grid sees the cells that overlap its window, which may be none.
    public func forEachSeed(near p: Vector3, _ visit: (Int, Vector3) -> Void) {
        func cell(_ value: Double, _ count: Int) -> Int { Int(min(max((value / radius).rounded(.down), -2), Double(count + 1))) }
        let (cx, cy, cz) = (cell(p.x - origin.x, counts[0]), cell(p.y - origin.y, counts[1]), cell(p.z - origin.z, counts[2]))
        for z in cz - 1...cz + 1 where z >= 0 && z < counts[2] {
            for y in cy - 1...cy + 1 where y >= 0 && y < counts[1] {
                for x in cx - 1...cx + 1 where x >= 0 && x < counts[0] {
                    let index = (z * counts[1] + y) * counts[0] + x
                    for entry in Int(starts[index])..<Int(starts[index + 1]) {
                        let seed = Int(entries[entry])
                        visit(seed, points[seed])
                    }
                }
            }
        }
    }

    /// The distance to the nearest seed within the window of `p`, or `nil` when there is none (a seed farther than `radius` may be
    /// missed, which is the point: the lattice reads a hole there).
    public func nearestDistance(to p: Vector3) -> Double? {
        var nearest: Double?
        forEachSeed(near: p) { _, position in nearest = min(nearest ?? .infinity, (p - position).length) }
        return nearest
    }

    /// The fraction of a `steps`³ grid of points in `box` that is farther than three quarters of `radius` from every seed: where the
    /// lattice would be solid because the seeds don't reach (implicit spec §3.4's gaps). Poisson-like seeds from `searchRadius` leave
    /// essentially none.
    public func uncoveredFraction(in box: BoundingBox, steps: Int = 12) -> Double {
        var uncovered = 0
        for i in 0..<steps {
            for j in 0..<steps {
                for k in 0..<steps {
                    let fraction = Vector3(Double(i) + 0.5, Double(j) + 0.5, Double(k) + 0.5) * (1 / Double(steps))
                    let point = box.min + Vector3(box.size.x * fraction.x, box.size.y * fraction.y, box.size.z * fraction.z)
                    if (nearestDistance(to: point) ?? .infinity) > 0.75 * radius { uncovered += 1 }
                }
            }
        }
        return Double(uncovered) / Double(steps * steps * steps)
    }

    public var estimatedBytes: Int { points.count * 24 + starts.count * 4 + entries.count * 4 }

    public static func == (lhs: VoronoiSeeds, rhs: VoronoiSeeds) -> Bool { lhs === rhs }
    public func hash(into hasher: inout Hasher) { hasher.combine(ObjectIdentifier(self)) }
}

    ///   - lattice: the lattice, already cut to the region's box.
    ///   - skin: the wall thickness in millimetres, 0 for none.
    static func fill(region: ImplicitSolid, lattice: ImplicitSolid, skin: Double) -> ImplicitSolid {
        guard skin > 0 else { return .intersect([region, lattice], blend: .constant(0)) }
        let wall = ImplicitSolid.shell(region, thickness: .constant(skin), side: .inward)
        let inside = ImplicitSolid.offset(region, distance: .constant(-skin))
        return .union([wall, .intersect([lattice, inside], blend: .constant(0))], blend: .constant(0))
    }

        return region
    }

    /// The `skin`, in millimetres.
    static func skin(_ inputs: NodeInputs) throws -> Double {
        let skin = try inputs.number("skin")
        guard skin.isFinite, skin >= 0 else { throw NodeError.invalidValue("“skin” must be 0 or more.") }
        return skin
    }

    /// The `cell size` field and the `cell stretch` of a lattice over `box`.
    static func cells(_ inputs: NodeInputs, over box: BoundingBox, kernel: any Kernel,
                      context: EvalContext) async throws -> (cells: LatticeCells, warnings: [String]) {
        let stretch = try inputs.vector("cell stretch")
        guard stretch.isFinite, stretch.x > 0, stretch.y > 0, stretch.z > 0 else {

        }

                                                     over: ImplicitGrading.region(around: box), holdsAtZero: false, kernel: kernel,
                                                     context: context)

            throw NodeError.invalidValue("“cell size” must stay above 0 mm; it reaches \(smallest.millimetreText(locale: .messages)) mm.")
        }

    }

    static func grading(_ inputs: NodeInputs, _ name: SocketName, over box: BoundingBox, kernel: any Kernel,
                        context: EvalContext) async throws -> ImplicitGrading.Result {
        try await ImplicitGrading.make(try inputs.field(name), named: name, over: ImplicitGrading.region(around: box), holdsAtZero: true,
                                       kernel: kernel, context: context)
    }

    static func voxel(over box: BoundingBox, context: EvalContext) -> (size: Double, name: String) {

    public static let typeID = "creator.tpms"
    public static let displayName = "TPMS"
    public static let category = NodeCategory.implicit
    public static let inputs = [
        SocketSpec("region", .implicit, optional: true),
        SocketSpec("skin", .number, defaultValue: .number(0), unit: .millimetres, range: 0...20),
        SocketSpec("type", .integer, defaultValue: .integer(TPMSType.gyroid.rawValue)),
        SocketSpec("form", .integer, defaultValue: .integer(TPMSForm.sheet.rawValue)),
        SocketSpec("cell size", .field, defaultValue: .number(8), unit: .millimetres, range: 1...50),
        SocketSpec("cell stretch", .vector, defaultValue: .vector(Vector3(1, 1, 1))),
        SocketSpec("thickness", .field, defaultValue: .number(1), unit: .millimetres, range: 0...10),
    ]
    public static let outputs = [SocketSpec("implicit", .implicit)]
    public static let help = "Where the cell size field changes fast the pattern stretches, and the preview gets slower: the "
        + "lipschitz bound grows with the field's slope. A network's thickness moves the level, so 0 fills half the region."
    public static let inspector = [
        InspectorSection(title: "Surface", controls: [
            .segmented("type", options: TPMSType.options), .segmented("form", options: TPMSForm.options),
        ]),
        InspectorSection(title: "Cells", controls: [.number("cell size"), .vector("cell stretch")]),
        InspectorSection(title: "Wall", controls: [.number("thickness"), .number("skin"), .note(help)]),
    ]

    public static func evaluate(_ inputs: NodeInputs, kernel: any Kernel, context: EvalContext) async throws -> NodeOutputs {
        let region = try LatticeSetup.region(inputs)
        let skin = try LatticeSetup.skin(inputs)
        let type = TPMSType(rawValue: try inputs.choice("type", options: TPMSType.options)) ?? .gyroid
        let form = TPMSForm(rawValue: try inputs.choice("form", options: TPMSForm.options)) ?? .sheet
        let box = region.bounds
        let (cells, cellWarnings) = try await LatticeSetup.cells(inputs, over: box, kernel: kernel, context: context)
        let wall = try await LatticeSetup.grading(inputs, "thickness", over: box, kernel: kernel, context: context)

        warnings += ImplicitReach.warnings(for: region, reachedBy: skin)
        let result = LatticeFill.fill(region: region, lattice: lattice, skin: skin)
        return NodeOutputs(["implicit": .implicit(result.reference)], warnings: warnings)
    }

import CreatorGeometry
import CreatorGraph
import CreatorImplicit
import CreatorKernel

/// Fills a region with the cell edges (Struts) or the cell walls (Foam) of a Voronoi diagram (implicit spec §3.4). The seeds are a
/// point list from any source (a vector or a plane's origin), or, when `seeds` is unconnected, `count` built-in seeds placed by
/// `density` and `seed` (`VoronoiSeedSource`). `thickness` is the strut diameter or the wall thickness, a field.
public enum VoronoiLatticeNode: NodeDefinition {
    public static let typeID = "creator.voronoiLattice"
    public static let displayName = "Voronoi Lattice"
    public static let category = NodeCategory.implicit
    public static let inputs = [
        SocketSpec("region", .implicit, optional: true),
        SocketSpec("skin", .number, defaultValue: .number(0), unit: .millimetres, range: 0...20),
        SocketSpec("seeds", .plane, access: .list, optional: true),
        SocketSpec("form", .integer, defaultValue: .integer(VoronoiForm.struts.rawValue)),
        SocketSpec("thickness", .field, defaultValue: .number(0.8), unit: .millimetres, range: 0...10),
        SocketSpec("count", .integer, defaultValue: .integer(100), unit: .count),
        SocketSpec("density", .field, defaultValue: .number(1), range: 0...1),
        SocketSpec("seed", .integer, defaultValue: .integer(0)),
    ]
    public static let outputs = [SocketSpec("implicit", .implicit)]
    public static let help = "Wire points into “seeds”, or leave it unconnected for “count” seeds placed at random where “density” "
        + "(a field, 0 to 1) says. Changing the seed or the density moves every seed."
    public static let inspector = [
        InspectorSection(title: "Form", controls: [.segmented("form", options: VoronoiForm.options), .number("thickness")]),
        InspectorSection(title: "Seeds", controls: [.integer("count"), .number("density"), .integer("seed"), .note(help)]),
        InspectorSection(title: "Skin", controls: [.number("skin")]),
    ]

    public static func evaluate(_ inputs: NodeInputs, kernel: any Kernel, context: EvalContext) async throws -> NodeOutputs {
        let region = try LatticeSetup.region(inputs)
        let skin = try LatticeSetup.skin(inputs)
        let form = VoronoiForm(rawValue: try inputs.choice("form", options: VoronoiForm.options)) ?? .struts
        let box = region.bounds
        let wall = try await LatticeSetup.grading(inputs, "thickness", over: box, kernel: kernel, context: context)
        let (points, seedWarnings) = try await seeds(inputs, box: box, kernel: kernel, context: context)

        let seedGrid = VoronoiSeeds(points: points, radius: VoronoiSeeds.searchRadius(count: points.count, in: box))
        let lattice = ImplicitSolid.voronoi(VoronoiLattice(seeds: seedGrid, form: form, thickness: wall.grading, region: box))
        var warnings = seedWarnings + wall.warnings
        if seedGrid.uncoveredFraction(in: box) > 0 {
            warnings.append("The seeds leave gaps in the region; the lattice is solid there.")
        }
        let voxel = LatticeSetup.voxel(over: box, context: context)
        warnings += LatticeSetup.cellWarnings(spacing: seedGrid.radius / 2, voxel: voxel)
        let noun = form == .struts ? "Struts" : "Walls"
        warnings += LatticeSetup.thinWarnings(noun, thickest: wall.grading.range.upperBound, voxel: voxel)
        warnings += ImplicitReach.warnings(for: region, reachedBy: skin)
        let result = LatticeFill.fill(region: region, lattice: lattice, skin: skin)
        return NodeOutputs(["implicit": .implicit(result.reference)], warnings: warnings)
    }

    /// The seeds: the wired points, else the built-in ones.
    private static func seeds(_ inputs: NodeInputs, box: BoundingBox, kernel: any Kernel,
                              context: EvalContext) async throws -> (points: [Vector3], warnings: [String]) {
        let limit = VoronoiSeedSource.maximumCount
        guard inputs.has("seeds") else {
            let count = try inputs.integer("count")
            guard (1...limit).contains(count) else {
                throw NodeError.invalidValue("“count” must be between 1 and \(limit.display).")
            }
            let made = try await VoronoiSeedSource.points(count: count, density: try inputs.field("density"),
                                                          seed: try inputs.integer("seed"), in: box, kernel: kernel,
                                                          cache: context.fieldDistances)
            return (made.points, made.warnings)
        }
        let items = try inputs.list("seeds")
        guard !items.isEmpty else {
            throw NodeError.invalidValue("“seeds” has no points. Wire some in, or disconnect it to use “count” seeds.")
        }
        guard items.count <= limit else {
            throw NodeError.invalidValue("A Voronoi lattice takes at most \(limit.display) seeds; “seeds” has \(items.count.display).")
        }
        let points = try items.map { item -> Vector3 in
            switch item {
            case .vector(let point): point
            case .plane(let plane): plane.origin
            default: throw NodeError.typeMismatch("seeds", expected: .plane)
            }

import CreatorGeometry
import CreatorGraph
import CreatorImplicit
import CreatorKernel
import Foundation

/// The built-in seeds of a Voronoi lattice (implicit spec §3.4): `count` points drawn uniformly through a box, each kept with the
/// probability the `density` field gives at it (clamped to 0 ... 1), until there are `count` of them.
///
/// Candidate `i` is drawn from `SeededRandom(seed:, purpose: .voronoi)` alone: its position from draws 4i to 4i + 2 and its
/// acceptance from draw 4i + 3. The seeds are the first `count` accepted candidates, in order, so a seed depends on the
/// `seed`, the density and the box, never on how the candidates were batched, and raising `count` keeps the seeds already there.
enum VoronoiSeedSource {
    static let maximumCount = 100_000
    /// The most candidates tried per seed wanted before the search gives up on a density that is almost 0.
    static let attemptsPerSeed = 100

    struct Result {
        var points: [Vector3]
        var warnings: [String]
    }

    /// The box the seeds fill for a region in `box`: the region grown by half the mean spacing, so the cells at its edge are real.
    static func area(for box: BoundingBox, count: Int) -> BoundingBox {
        let volume = max(box.size.x, 1e-3) * max(box.size.y, 1e-3) * max(box.size.z, 1e-3)
        return ImplicitSolid.grown(box, by: cbrt(volume / Double(max(count, 1))) / 2)
    }

    static func points(count: Int, density: Field, seed: Int, in box: BoundingBox, kernel: any Kernel,
                       cache: FieldDistanceCache) async throws -> Result {
        let area = area(for: box, count: count)
        let random = SeededRandom(seed: seed, purpose: .voronoi)
        var points: [Vector3] = []
        points.reserveCapacity(count)
        var next = 0
        let limit = attemptsPerSeed * count + 10_000
        while points.count < count && next < limit {
            try Task.checkCancellation()
            let wanted = count - points.count
            // Expect the acceptance seen so far to continue; the first batch is exactly what is wanted.
            let rate = next == 0 ? 1 : max(Double(points.count) / Double(next), 0.01)
            let size = min(max(Int((Double(wanted) * 1.5 / rate).rounded(.up)), 256), 200_000, limit - next)
            let candidates = (next..<next + size).map { index in
                area.min + Vector3(area.size.x * random.unit(at: 4 * index), area.size.y * random.unit(at: 4 * index + 1),
                                   area.size.z * random.unit(at: 4 * index + 2))
            }
            let values: [Double]
            if case .constant(let level) = density {
                values = [Double](repeating: level, count: size)
            } else {
                values = try await FieldSampler.sample(density, at: candidates, kernel: kernel, cache: cache)
            }
            for (offset, value) in values.enumerated() {
                let chance = value.isFinite ? min(max(value, 0), 1) : 0
                guard random.unit(at: 4 * (next + offset) + 3) < chance else { continue }
                points.append(candidates[offset])
                if points.count == count {
                    next += offset + 1
                    break
                }
            }
            if points.count < count { next += size }
        }
        var warnings: [String] = []
        if points.isEmpty {
            throw NodeError.invalidValue("The density is 0 everywhere in the region, so there are no seeds.")
        }
        if points.count < count {
            warnings.append("The density left room for only \(points.count.display) of the \(count.display) seeds.")
        }
        return Result(points: points, warnings: warnings)
    }
}

struct LatticeMeshTests {

        var struts: Node
        var voronoi: Node
        /// The Mesh node and the Output of each lattice, in the order TPMS, Strut Lattice, Voronoi Lattice.
        var meshes: [Node]
        var outputs: [Node]
    }

    /// A 20 mm cube filled by each lattice in turn, each into a Mesh at `resolution` and an Output.
    func graph(_ h: inout Harness, skin: Double = 0, resolution: Double = 1) -> Lattices {
        let cube = h.add(ImplicitBoxNode.self, ["size": .vector(Vector3(20, 20, 20))])
        let tpms = h.add(TPMSNode.self, ["cell size": .number(10), "thickness": .number(0.8), "skin": .number(skin)])
        let struts = h.add(StrutLatticeNode.self, [
            "cell": .integer(StrutCell.bcc.rawValue), "cell size": .number(10), "strut radius": .number(1.2), "skin": .number(skin),
        ])
        let voronoi = h.add(VoronoiLatticeNode.self, [
            "count": .integer(30), "form": .integer(VoronoiForm.foam.rawValue), "thickness": .number(1), "skin": .number(skin),
        ])
        var meshes: [Node] = [], outputs: [Node] = []
        for lattice in [tpms, struts, voronoi] {
            h.wire(cube, "implicit", to: lattice, "region")

            let output = h.add(OutputNode.self, output: true)
            h.wire(lattice, "implicit", to: mesh, "implicit")
            h.wire(mesh, "mesh", to: output, "mesh")
            meshes.append(mesh)
            outputs.append(output)

## Risks

- **The i-4 budget (an estimate, not a measurement).** Rows i-1 to i-4 were never run on the new gyroid. The gyroid fixture is about 19.6 million triangles at 0.1 mm (measured at 1.0 mm: 196,128). At 7c-1's 155 bytes a triangle that is 3.0 GB against the 4 GB budget, and the time scaled from 7c-1's run is about 55 s against 60 s, on a loaded Mac. A miss is recorded as a miss; D12's streaming writer (7c-3) is the fix. Fallback if peak memory is over 4 GB: the 50 mm cell (U7 b).
- **Lipschitz margins are empirical** (decision 10): a surface whose steepest slope sits between a 160³ grid's samples could exceed its bound by under 5%; the property test and the bounds test would show it on the fixtures, and the cost of an overestimate is speed only.
- **A graded `density` leaves a slab** (U5): where a ramp reaches 0 the built-in seeds are sparser than the search radius, the lattice is solid there and the node warns; it is pinned, not avoided.
- **The strut warning is a floor** (Review Focus 6): lattices of some cells read solid from about 0.71 of a cell, below the 0.85 that warns.
- **Voronoi Struts are not a uniform radius** (U4): the radius is exact where three equal cells meet and varies with the seeds' regularity.
- **A Metal compile of the new shader text happened only on this Mac** (the agreement suite compiles and runs all 39 fixtures); `precise::sin` is used for the trigonometry, and a different GPU's compiler is untested.
- **Debug-build test time.** `LatticeMeshTests` takes about 15 s on a loaded Mac in a debug build (CPU meshing of small cubes).
