# Adopt MetalUI C22 (window drag) and the hidden title bar Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Turn on the hidden title bar (gap M6-c) and make the top bar's empty space, and a strip above it, drag the window with MetalUI C22's `WindowDragGesture` (gap C8-a), with every handle beside the controls rather than behind them, and without touching the viewport's and canvas's gestures.

**Architecture:** One public view, `WindowDragHandle` (`Color.clear` + `contentShape` + `.gesture(WindowDragGesture())`), in `CreatorStyle` so both `CreatorApp` and `CreatorSketchEditor` can use it. It fills the gaps beside the top bar's controls (the label, the space before Preview, the sketch toolbar's label and the gap before Finish) and `AppRoot` lays one across the window's top for `AppLayout.windowDragStripHeight`, above the panels (so the sketch chrome's backdrop cannot take the press) and below the pick banner and the palette. The window opens with `AppLayout.windowStyle` (`.hiddenTitleBar`), a constant `main.swift` and the tests' `AppWindow` share; `TopBar`'s existing clearance (`AppLayout.topBarClearance`) keeps the content clear of the window buttons and is now tested through the real root with the platform's insets set.

**Tech Stack:** Swift 6.4 strict, Swift Testing, MetalUI (`WindowDragGesture`, `PlatformWindow.performWindowDrag`, `MetalUITesting`'s `TestWindow.windowDrags`), SwiftLint strict.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-metalcreator-vertical-slice-design.md` §6.1 (window layout: the glass top bar is drawn under the window buttons) and its Errata (M6), which this plan updates; `docs/metalui-gaps.md` entries M6-c and C8-a; `docs/verification/human-checks.md` group AS (AS-5); MetalUI's `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-12-window-drag-design.md` (item C22, rulings WD-A..WD-K, on master `930db4a`, read only).

## Review Focus

The spec says the glass top bar sits under the window buttons and says nothing about what a press does there. Most likely to bite, each with the test that pins it:

1. **A press-drag that starts on a button, the Preview picker or Export moves the window.** Someone reaches for Undo, wobbles, and the window flies. Measured on this tree, an enabled control keeps its press even with a handle behind the whole bar (0 requests for Preview, Export, and Undo and Redo with a step to take back), but a disabled control has no hitbox, so its press falls through to a handle behind it (the fresh document's Undo and Redo give 1). Handles therefore sit beside the controls (Decision 2). Tests: `WindowDragWindowTests.aPressDragOnABarControlNeverAsks`, `SketchWindowDragWindowTests.noToolbarButtonMovesTheWindow`, `everyControlStartsBelowTheStrip`.
2. **A click in the bar or the strip changes the viewport's selection, or a drag in them orbits the camera.** Before this plan a click on the bar's empty glass reached the viewport (glass has no hitbox). Now the band holds the press. Tests: `aClickInTheBandDoesNotReachTheViewportAndOneBelowDoes`, `aDragStartedInTheBarLeavesTheViewportAlone`; and a drag just under the bar still orbits: `aDragBelowTheBarStillOrbitsTheViewportAndDoesNotAsk`, `thePaddingBelowTheControlsIsNotAHandle`.
3. **A double click or a right-click in the bar zooms or moves the window, or opens the viewport's menu.** Tests: `aSecondaryPressOrADoubleClickDoesNotMoveOrZoomTheWindow` (no window drag, `titleBarPresses` empty).
4. **The content under the window buttons: full screen, a standard window, the minimum width, a sketch.** In full screen AppKit reports zero insets and the content must move back; a standard-style window must not pad; at 1000 pt nothing overlaps. Tests: `TitleBarClearanceWindowTests` (all seven; they land in Task 2, so Task 3's commit is the `main.swift` line alone). The sketch toolbar at 1000 pt is squeezed (its tool buttons shrink and the gap before Finish is almost gone; checked, not pinned): human check AS-9.
5. **The platform declines the drag (SDL, or AppKit outside a drag).** The press must still be claimed, or the viewport would orbit under the bar. Test: `whenThePlatformDeclinesThePressIsStillClaimed`.

Human-only (the real AppKit drag, release outside the window, ⌘-Tab mid-drag, full screen, a second display): AS-5, AS-8, AS-9, AS-10.

## Decisions made in this plan

1. **`WindowDragHandle` lives in `CreatorStyle`, public.** `CreatorApp` (the top bar, `AppRoot`) and `CreatorSketchEditor` (the sketch toolbar) both depend on `CreatorStyle` and on nothing else in common; `GlassPanel` is in `CreatorEditor`, which `CreatorSketchEditor` does not import.
2. **Handles sit beside controls, not behind them.** A product choice (User decision 1), not a MetalUI workaround. An earlier draft of this plan logged a gap, C22-a, claiming that a press-drag from a control above a handle moves the window; re-measured in a scratch copy (a `WindowDragHandle` behind the whole `HStack`, then behind the whole `TopBar` in `AppRoot`; `TestWindow.drag` 40 points right from the centre of each control), it does not: Preview, Export, and an enabled Undo and Redo give 0 requests, the gap gives 1. Only a disabled control passes the press to the handle behind it. So there is no gap to log or forward. Consequence of the choice: the bar's 10-pt glass padding below and between the controls does not drag.
3. **The strip is `margin + glassPadding` = 22 pt tall, full window width, drawn above the panels** (after the `VStack` that holds the bar and the panels; below the pick banner, the palette and the library drag overlay). It covers the margin above the bar and the glass's top padding, ending exactly where the controls start (`everyControlStartsBelowTheStrip` pins that no control starts above it), so lying over the chrome costs no control a press. Above the panels is deliberate: in a sketch, `SketchChrome`'s backdrop (`Color.clear` + `contentShape` + `DragGesture(minimumDistance: 0)`) starts at the glass, y = 12, and would take a press at y 12..22 if the strip were beneath it, so only the top 12 pt would drag; the plain bar's glass is paint-only and would have dragged there. Now all 22 pt drag in both modes (`theToolbarsLabelGapAndStripMoveTheWindow` presses at y = 5, 15 and 21). The traffic-light row (the window buttons draw above it natively) is inside it.
4. **`SketchChrome` is untouched** (c21 may edit it). The sketch toolbar's handles are drawn above its `DragGesture(minimumDistance: 0)` backdrop; measured: the handle wins, the buttons still click, the inspector (same chrome) stays inert. The strip reaches over it from `AppRoot` (decision 3).
5. **Window style as one constant.** `AppLayout.windowStyle = WindowStyle.hiddenTitleBar` is read by `main.swift` and by `AppWindow.open`, so the tests' window cannot drift from the app's. `main.swift` is an executable; a source-reading test (the precedent is `PackagingScriptTests`) pins that its one line is there.
6. **The hidden title bar is its own task and commit (Task 3)**: the `main.swift` line and `ExecutableWindowStyleTests`, its source-reading test, and nothing else. A revert of that one commit puts the standard title bar back and removes only that test. The clearance suite (`TitleBarClearanceWindowTests`, seven tests, which run the real root with the platform's insets set and do not need the line) is in Task 2's commit and stays, as do the handles (harmless in a standard window). See User decisions, 3.
7. **Tests drive the real root.** `AppWindow` (MetalUITesting) opens `AppWindowRoot`; a press-drag is `TestWindow.drag(from:to:)`; the answer is `TestWindow.windowDrags` (the platform being asked), plus the viewport's `pose`. The platform's insets are set with `HeadlessPlatformWindow.titleBarInsets` + a resize report, which is what AppKit does.

## User decisions

Product behaviour the spec leaves open. Each is written for the recommended option.

1. **Which regions drag the window.** Options: (a) the top bar's empty space (the name and the gap beside the controls, in both the bar and the sketch toolbar) plus a 22-pt strip along the window's top; (b) the whole glass bar, including its padding and the gaps between controls (buildable now with no MetalUI change: a handle behind the bar leaves enabled controls working, measured for the top bar; a disabled Undo or Redo would then drag the window; the sketch toolbar would need the same measurement; `TopBar` gets `.background { WindowDragHandle() }` on its `GlassPanel`, and `thePaddingBelowTheControlsIsNotAHandle` is inverted); (c) (a) plus empty header areas of the graph panel and inspector (more to grab, but they carry their own gestures and the panels float over the viewport, so a mistaken drag moves the window instead of the panel's content); (d) the strip only. **Recommended: (a).** It is the layout already measured and tested for both bars, it leaves `SketchChrome` and the glass alone (c21 may edit them), and controls are never handles; (b) is the natural follow-up if the dead padding annoys in AS-8. Written as (a).
2. **Double-click on the band.** Options: (a) nothing; (b) zoom the window as a standard title bar does. MetalUI's `WindowDragGesture` does not zoom, and SwiftUI's does not either; MetalUI's own title-bar press (`performTitleBarPress`) is only asked for an unclaimed press, which the handles now claim, so (b) would need a MetalUI change (a new gap) and is not buildable in the app today. **Recommended: (a).** Written as (a); `aSecondaryPressOrADoubleClickDoesNotMoveOrZoomTheWindow` pins it, and AS-5 records what a real double click does.
3. **Merge the hidden title bar before AS-5 has been run?** An earlier decision (AS "Product decisions", 1, approved by the user 2026-10-09/10) said it ships only if AS-5 shows the window drags. Option (a) changes that approved wording to "ships now, stays only if AS-5 passes"; Task 4 records it as an amendment pending this answer, not as already approved. The task here says to adopt C22 and mark M6-c and C8-a "adopted (pending AS-5)". Options: (a) merge now, marked pending; if AS-5 fails, revert Task 3's one commit (the `windowStyle:` line and its source-reading test) and the standard title bar returns, leaving the handles harmless; (b) hold the whole branch until AS-5. **Recommended: (a)**, since Task 3 is separable. Written as (a).
4. **A click on the bar's empty space no longer reaches the viewport** (it used to deselect, because the glass has no hitbox). Options: (a) the bar holds the click (nothing happens); (b) let a plain click through to the viewport. **Recommended: (a)**: a press on window chrome should not change the selection, and (b) cannot be built with a gesture-carrying handle. Written as (a); pinned by `aClickInTheBandDoesNotReachTheViewportAndOneBelowDoes`.

Create `Tests/CreatorAppTests/Support/AppWindow+Drag.swift`:

````swift
// Test fixture file: points and counts for the window-drag tests.
import MetalUI
import MetalUITesting
import Testing
@testable import CreatorApp

extension AppWindow {
    /// A window point.
    func point(_ x: Float, _ y: Float) -> Point<Pixels> {
        Point(x: Pixels(x), y: Pixels(y))
    }

    /// The middle of `element`'s frame.
    func centre(of element: TestElement) -> Point<Pixels> {
        point(element.frame.origin.x.value + element.frame.size.width.value / 2,
              element.frame.origin.y.value + element.frame.size.height.value / 2)
    }

    /// How many times MetalUI asked the platform to move the window for a 40-point press-drag to the right from `start`.
    func windowDragRequests(draggingFrom start: Point<Pixels>) -> Int {
        let before = window.windowDrags
        window.drag(from: start, to: point(start.x.value + 40, start.y.value))
        return window.windowDrags - before
    }

    /// The top bar's pressable controls (buttons, the Preview picker, the Export menu), found by their frames above the
    /// bar's lower edge, so the graph panel's and the view cube's buttons are left out.
    func topBarControls() throws -> [TestElement] {
        let bottom = Float(AppLayout.margin + AppLayout.topBarHeight)
        let roles: [AccessibilityRole] = [.button, .popUpButton, .menuButton]
        return try window.elements(where: { roles.contains($0.role) && $0.frame.origin.y.value < bottom })
    }

    /// The top bar's label: the document's name or "Sketch".
    func topBarLabel() throws -> TestElement {
        let bottom = Float(AppLayout.margin + AppLayout.topBarHeight)
        let labels = try window.elements(where: { $0.role == .staticText && $0.frame.origin.y.value < bottom })
        return try #require(labels.first)
    }
}
````

Create `Tests/CreatorAppTests/WindowDragWindowTests.swift`:

````swift
import CreatorGeometry
import MetalUI
import MetalUITesting
import Testing
@testable import CreatorApp
@testable import CreatorViewport

/// The window is dragged by its top bar (spec §6.1, gap M6-c, MetalUI C22): a press-drag on the bar's empty space, or on
/// the strip above it, asks the platform window to move (`performWindowDrag`, counted by the headless window); a press
/// on a control never does, and the viewport beneath is as it was. Whether AppKit really moves the window is human
/// check AS-5.
@MainActor
struct WindowDragWindowTests {
    /// The gap between the name and the Preview picker, in the bar's middle.
    var gap: Point<Pixels> { Point(x: Pixels(600), y: Pixels(34)) }
    /// The margin above the bar, across the window.
    var strip: Point<Pixels> { Point(x: Pixels(600), y: Pixels(5)) }
    /// Above the graph panel with the default (bottom) dock: the viewport.
    var viewportSpot: Point<Pixels> { Point(x: Pixels(700), y: Pixels(300)) }

    @Test func aPressDragOnTheBarsEmptySpaceAsksThePlatformToMoveTheWindow() async throws {
        let app = try await AppWindow.open()
        #expect(app.windowDragRequests(draggingFrom: gap) == 1)
        #expect(app.windowDragRequests(draggingFrom: app.point(30, 34)) == 1, "and on the document's name")
    }

    @Test func aPressDragOnTheStripAboveTheBarAsksToo() async throws {
        let app = try await AppWindow.open()
        #expect(app.windowDragRequests(draggingFrom: strip) == 1)
        #expect(app.windowDragRequests(draggingFrom: app.point(600, Float(AppLayout.margin) + 3)) == 1, "over the glass's padding")
        let lastRow = Float(AppLayout.windowDragStripHeight) - 1
        #expect(app.windowDragRequests(draggingFrom: app.point(600, lastRow)) == 1, "down to where the controls start")
    }

    @Test func aPressDragOnABarControlNeverAsks() async throws {
        let app = try await AppWindow.open()
        let controls = try app.topBarControls()
        #expect(controls.count == 4, "Preview, Undo, Redo and Export")
        for control in controls {
            #expect(app.windowDragRequests(draggingFrom: app.centre(of: control)) == 0, "\(control.label ?? "a control")")
        }
    }

    @Test func thePaddingBelowTheControlsIsNotAHandle() async throws {
        // By design (User decision 1): handles sit beside the controls, so the 10-point glass padding under them is not a
        // handle either. A press-drag there reaches the viewport beneath, as it always did.
        let app = try await AppWindow.open()
        let pose = app.model.viewport.pose
        let below = app.point(600, Float(AppLayout.margin + AppLayout.topBarHeight) - 4)
        #expect(app.windowDragRequests(draggingFrom: below) == 0)
        #expect(app.model.viewport.pose != pose)
    }

    @Test func aClickOnAControlStillClicks() async throws {
        let app = try await AppWindow.open()
        try app.window.click(try app.window.element(label: "Export"))
        #expect(app.window.platformWindow.presentedMenus.count == 1, "the Export menu opened")
        #expect(app.window.windowDrags == 0)
    }

    @Test func everyControlStartsBelowTheStrip() async throws {
        // A press on a control's top edge must not land in the strip, or it would drag the window.
        let app = try await AppWindow.open()
        let tops = try app.topBarControls().map(\.frame.origin.y.value)
        #expect(try #require(tops.min()) >= Float(AppLayout.windowDragStripHeight))
    }

    @Test func aDragBelowTheBarStillOrbitsTheViewportAndDoesNotAsk() async throws {
        let app = try await AppWindow.open()
        let pose = app.model.viewport.pose
        #expect(app.windowDragRequests(draggingFrom: viewportSpot) == 0)
        #expect(app.model.viewport.pose != pose, "the viewport's drag still moves the camera")
    }

    @Test func aDragStartedInTheBarLeavesTheViewportAlone() async throws {
        let app = try await AppWindow.open()
        let pose = app.model.viewport.pose
        for start in [gap, strip] {
            #expect(app.windowDragRequests(draggingFrom: start) == 1)
        }
        #expect(app.model.viewport.pose == pose, "the camera did not move")
        #expect(!app.model.viewport.isPointerDown && app.model.viewport.activeDragMode == nil)
    }

    @Test func aClickInTheBandDoesNotReachTheViewportAndOneBelowDoes() async throws {
        let app = try await AppWindow.open()
        let tool = ModifierProbeTool()
        app.model.viewport.tool = tool
        app.window.click(at: gap)
````

````swift
/// A region that moves the window when it is pressed and dragged (gap M6-c, MetalUI C22): SwiftUI's
/// `WindowDragGesture`. Under a hidden title bar there is no bar to grab, so the app marks the empty parts of its own

            .frame(maxWidth: .infinity, maxHeight: .infinity)
            .contentShape(Rectangle())
            .gesture(WindowDragGesture())
    }
}
````
